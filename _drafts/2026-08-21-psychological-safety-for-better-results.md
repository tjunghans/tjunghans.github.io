# Psychological Safey

Key ideas from Think Again about psychological safety include:

    People need to feel safe admitting mistakes, uncertainties, and changing their minds. Grant argues that rethinking is difficult when people fear being judged or punished for being wrong. [enotes.com]
    High-performing teams combine psychological safety with accountability. Safety alone is not enough; people should feel free to speak up while still being held to high standards. [enotes.com]
    Leaders help create safety by modeling vulnerability. Grant describes examples where leaders openly acknowledge weaknesses or receive criticism publicly, making it safer for others to do the same. [enotes.com], [assets.edg...gthink.com]
    Psychologically safe teams often report more errors because people are willing to disclose them, but they tend to learn faster and make fewer serious mistakes over time. Grant cites Amy Edmondson's research to make this point. [enotes.com]
    A culture of learning requires people to challenge assumptions and voice dissenting views without fear of reprisal. This is a recurring theme in his discussion of rethinking within organizations. [enotes.com], [safesystems.com]
